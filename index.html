When you can't call for help, your wrist does.

Vigil runs on your Fitbit Versa and watches for the physiological signature of a cardiac emergency. If something looks wrong and you don't respond, it notifies your next of kin and emergency services with your location and medical profile — in under a minute, without you touching anything.

How it works

Detect, confirm, escalate.

The hard part of automatic emergency alerting isn't detection — it's not crying wolf. Vigil never dials on a single reading. Every alert passes through a confirmation step that you can cancel.

STEP 01

Detect

The optical sensor streams heart rate and rhythm continuously. Vigil flags sustained abnormality — not a single spike — and cross-checks it against the accelerometer.

Abnormal rhythm + body at rest
STEP 02

Confirm

The watch vibrates hard and asks if you're okay, then starts a 30-second countdown. One tap cancels it and nothing leaves the device. Silence is treated as an answer.

30s cancellable countdown
STEP 03

Escalate

The paired phone calls emergency services and your next of kin, transmits GPS location, and hands over your medical profile — blood type, medications, allergies.

Location + medical profile sent
What it watches

One signal lies. Five together don't.

A racing heart rate means nothing on its own — you could be running for a bus. Vigil only escalates when several independent signals agree, and motion context is what separates a sprint from an emergency.

Resting rate anomaly

Heart rate far outside your personal baseline while the accelerometer reports no movement. The single strongest false-positive filter Vigil has.

PPG + Accelerometer

Rhythm irregularity

Beat-to-beat intervals that lose their regular pattern, consistent with arrhythmia rather than exertion.

PPG

Variability collapse

A sharp drop in heart rate variability, an early indicator of acute cardiac stress that often precedes symptoms.

PPG

Sudden bradycardia

An abrupt fall in heart rate. Cardiac events don't only present as a spike — a collapse in rate can matter just as much.

PPG

Oxygen desaturation

Falling blood-oxygen saturation alongside a rhythm anomaly raises confidence that circulation is genuinely compromised.

SpO₂

Collapse detection

An impact spike followed by complete stillness — the motion signature of someone who has fallen and not got back up.

Accelerometer
Your baseline

48 bpm is an emergency for one person and a Tuesday for another.

Population averages are why wearables cry wolf. Before Vigil watches anything, it asks about you — your history, your medication, your day — and derives every threshold on the watch from those answers instead of from a chart of everybody.

Who you are

  • How old are you?
  • Sex assigned at birth?
  • Height and weight?
Sets resting range & max-HR ceiling

A typical day

  • When do you usually sleep and wake?
  • Is your work seated, on your feet, or physical?
  • How often do you exercise, and how hard?
Sets when stillness is expected

Medical history

  • Any diagnosed heart, lung or metabolic conditions?
  • Ever had a cardiac event, fainting episode or seizure?
  • Anything similar in your immediate family?
Sets which patterns get weighted up

Medications

  • A beta blocker or other rate-controlling drug?
  • Stimulants — ADHD medication, decongestants?
  • Insulin or other glucose-lowering medication?
Corrects for drugs that move heart rate on purpose

Food through the day

  • When do you normally eat, and do you skip meals?
  • How much caffeine, and how late?
  • Do you go long stretches without eating?
Explains post-meal and fasting shifts

Alcohol & tobacco

  • Do you drink? How often, and how much?
  • Do you smoke or vape?
  • Anything else we should know about?
Accounts for known effects on rate & variability

Same reading, different verdict. A pulse of 108 at three in the afternoon is unremarkable for this person — he's just walked somewhere. The same 108 at three in the morning, with no movement for twenty minutes and variability under 22 ms, is three signals disagreeing with his own baseline at once. A population threshold would have missed it, or fired on it every afternoon.

His diabetes doesn't give Vigil a glucose reading — nothing on the watch can do that. It changes what the watch does with a reading it already has: an unexplained rate spike with tremor and no motion is worth surfacing sooner in someone on glucose-lowering medication than in someone who isn't.

Not yet — and here's what's missing.

The intake collects all of this today, because the answers are worth having and because we'd rather build the profile once. The watch can't act on these yet. Saying so here seemed better than implying otherwise.

Blood pressure spikes The Versa's optical sensor trends pulse, not pressure. Cuffless blood pressure from the wrist isn't accurate enough to raise an alarm on, and a hypertensive crisis is precisely the case where a wrong number does harm. Needs a paired cuff or validated device
Blood sugar Vigil can record that you're diabetic and on insulin, and it can notice the racing pulse, sweating and tremor that often accompany a hypo. It cannot tell you your glucose. That number has to come from a continuous monitor. Needs a paired CGM — Dexcom, Libre
Anaphylactic shock Here the gap isn't a sensor. The profile already records severe allergies, and the pattern — rate climbing, oxygen falling, skin response spiking — is within reach of the hardware. What we don't have is evidence that we can separate it from the other things that look like it, confidently enough to dial on. Needs validation data, not hardware
Being honest

What Vigil is not.

A tool that can summon an ambulance has to be honest about what it can't do. These are real constraints, not disclaimers we're hiding at the bottom.

It cannot diagnose a heart attack.A wrist optical sensor is not an ECG and cannot identify a myocardial infarction. Vigil detects patterns of physiological distress and unresponsiveness — that is a different, more modest claim.
It is not a certified medical device.No regulatory clearance. It does not replace medical advice, and it must never be a reason to delay calling emergency services yourself.
False positives are possible.This is why the countdown exists and why we gate on stillness. An unnecessary ambulance dispatch is a real cost to a real emergency system, and we design against it rather than pretending it away.
It needs a phone in range.The watch detects; the paired phone places the call. Out of Bluetooth range or without signal, escalation cannot complete.
Your data stays yours.Vital signs are processed on-device. Location and medical profile are transmitted only during a confirmed escalation, and your next of kin must opt in before they can be contacted.
